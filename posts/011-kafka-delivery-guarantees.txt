Most developers use Kafka… but very few understand what guarantees it actually gives 🤔

At first glance, Apache Kafka feels simple:
producer → topic → consumer → done. But the tricky part is not how to use it — it’s what actually happens under the hood when things go wrong.

Let’s talk about delivery guarantees 👇

𝗔𝘁 𝗺𝗼𝘀𝘁 𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗲
Message is sent once… or maybe not at all.
Fast, but you can lose data.
𝗔𝘁 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝘀𝘁 𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗲
Message will be delivered, but duplicates are possible.
This is what most systems actually run on.
𝗘𝘅𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗹𝘆 𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗲
Sounds perfect. Almost never truly is.

Why? Because Kafka can guarantee exactly-once inside itself (producer → topic → consumer group),
but the moment you involve a database or external service — you’re back to reality.

Example:
– consumer reads message
– writes to DB
– crashes before committing offset

Kafka will resend the message → your DB now has duplicates. Kafka didn’t fail. Your assumptions did.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗽𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻
Instead of chasing “exactly-once”, strong systems do this:
– make consumers 𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗺𝗽𝗼𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁
– store processing state (eventId, status)
– commit offsets only after safe handling
– accept duplicates as a normal case

Because in distributed systems:
𝗗𝘂𝗽𝗹𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗲𝘀 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝗻 𝗹𝗼𝘀𝘀
And once you design with that in mind, Kafka becomes incredibly reliable.
