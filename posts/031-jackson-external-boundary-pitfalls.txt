No deploy on our side, no config change — yet every incoming webhook suddenly failed deserialization. The provider had added one field we did not even use, and Jackson refused to parse the payload.

Trap one, on by default: 𝗙𝗔𝗜𝗟_𝗢𝗡_𝗨𝗡𝗞𝗡𝗢𝗪𝗡_𝗣𝗥𝗢𝗣𝗘𝗥𝗧𝗜𝗘𝗦. The moment a contract you do not own grows a field your DTO has never seen, deserialization throws instead of ignoring it. That strictness is fine internally, but at a boundary you do not own it turns every additive change on their side into an incident on yours. External payloads must be read leniently.

Trap two is quieter, and scarier, because it never throws. Providers love introducing new status values, and the day they send one your enum has never seen, Jackson throws — so people reach for 𝗥𝗘𝗔𝗗_𝗨𝗡𝗞𝗡𝗢𝗪𝗡_𝗘𝗡𝗨𝗠_𝗩𝗔𝗟𝗨𝗘𝗦_𝗔𝗦_𝗡𝗨𝗟𝗟, which silently turns that status into 𝗻𝘂𝗹𝗹. Now a real payment carries a null status into your logic, your switch falls through, and a declined transaction is silently accepted. Far safer is an explicit @𝗝𝘀𝗼𝗻𝗘𝗻𝘂𝗺𝗗𝗲𝗳𝗮𝘂𝗹𝘁𝗩𝗮𝗹𝘂𝗲 mapped to 𝗨𝗡𝗞𝗡𝗢𝗪𝗡 that your code must handle on purpose.

Trap three hides inside numbers that look completely fine. When Jackson meets a JSON number like 𝟭𝟬.𝟭𝟬 and the target is a loosely typed field or 𝗢𝗯𝗷𝗲𝗰𝘁, it reads it as a 𝗱𝗼𝘂𝗯𝗹𝗲 — and you know where that ends for money, picking up binary floating point error before your code ever touches it. 𝗨𝗦𝗘_𝗕𝗜𝗚_𝗗𝗘𝗖𝗜𝗠𝗔𝗟_𝗙𝗢𝗥_𝗙𝗟𝗢𝗔𝗧𝗦 materializes floating point JSON straight into 𝗕𝗶𝗴𝗗𝗲𝗰𝗶𝗺𝗮𝗹, so amounts arrive exact instead of pre-corrupted the moment they cross the parser.

What actually held up for us 👇

• two mapper profiles — strict for our internal contracts, lenient for anything we do not control

• big-decimal-for-floats on everywhere money can appear, so precision loss never sneaks in at the edge

• an explicit unknown-enum default the code must handle, never a silent null standing in for a real state

• a contract test deserializing a payload with an extra unknown field, so "the provider added something" is caught in CI, not at 3am

The deeper lesson I keep relearning: deserialization is not plumbing, it is the boundary where a system you do not own reaches into yours. Its defaults decide what happens when the outside world changes shape — and if you never make that call on purpose, you have let an external team decide when your service breaks.
